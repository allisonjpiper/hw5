What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

Keeping numbers consistent across hand-offs is the hardest problem in this project, and a single agent wouldn't have it. In an earlier run of ticket 103, the price lived in Accounting's reasoning while the customer message was written by a different agent, and the shop briefly had two Yale AI Club quotes, one at $50 and one at $49.30. Fixing that took an extra rule (the Boss hands off one agent at a time) and a new behavior in save_message_draft (older drafts are superseded). One agent that set the $52 price and wrote the quote in the same conversation could never contradict itself. It would also be cheaper and simpler. On ticket 103, Accounting re-read price_bulk_order even though the Boss had just read it, and the whole ticket took 11 model calls across three agents where one agent could probably have done it in four or five. All of the routing machinery would disappear too: TICKET_PLANS, the depth limit, the no-loops rule and the follow-up-run instructions exist only to manage hand-offs. The trade-off is least privilege. Splitting the team is what guarantees that the agent writing customer messages can never touch pay_approved_request, and that only Accounting can queue money.

Three new problems the agents could not solve, and what they would need

The October rent gap

On 2026-10-02, Elm City Properties will expect another $2,400 for the Chapel Street shop, and checking holds $160. The agents now see this coming (get_cash_position warns about it), but they can't fix it. request_payment_approval won't queue more than the balance, pay_approved_request won't overdraw, and no tool can add money. Solving it would take a Treasury agent with three new tools: record_cash_inflow, so a human-approved sale like the $416 Yale AI Club order can actually raise the balance; forecast_cash_flow, to project the next 60 days of rent and invoices; and propose_payment_plan, to draft options for the owner. It would also need a human-gated send_message tool so Facilities could actually ask the landlord for a payment plan. Until then, the gap is a decision only the owner can make.

A misprinted reprint

Suppose the $840 Bulldog Print Co reprint arrives with the crest printed off-center. The agents have no way to see goods, so all they know is a database row saying invoice 501 is paid. They can't dispute the charge, because there's no refund tool and cash only moves out, and they can't contact the vendor. Solving it would take a Receiving agent with a receive_shipment tool where staff record the quantity and condition of what arrived, a record_vendor_credit tool that books a refund or credit only after a human confirms it, and an approved vendor-messaging tool so Inventory could ask Bulldog Print Co for a corrected run. Until then, the agents can only draft a complaint for a person to send.

A custom-logo hoodie order

The Yale AI Club comes back wanting 20 hoodies with its own logo printed on the back. Nothing about that exists in the shop's data: there's no sku, no unit cost for custom printing, and no Bulldog Print Co price list. Because every fact has to come from MCP, the agents can't invent a quote. Solving it would take a Merchandising agent with a request_vendor_quote tool (staff enter the vendor's quoted price and lead time), a create_custom_sku tool that adds the product to inventory and pricing once approved, and an artwork-approval step. It would also need a licensing check, a brand-policy table plus a human sign-off on whether a student group's logo can sit next to Yale branding, because no agent should make that call.
